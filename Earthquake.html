History of Destructive Earthquakes in Myanmar

28 March 2025
Mandalay-Sagaing
  • Magnitude: 7.7 Mw (according to the USGS)
  • Affected areas: Sagaing, Mandalay, and other parts of Myanmar
  • Impacts: Widespread collapse of buildings, bridges, and religious structures; thousands killed or injured; mass homelessness
24 August 2016
Chauk–Bagan
  • Magnitude: 6.8
  • Affected areas: Chauk, Bagan, and surrounding parts of central Myanmar
  • Impacts: 389 historical monuments and 17 small monuments damaged; major temples including Sulamani heavily affected
8 July 1975
Bagan
  • Magnitude: 6.5 (scientific earthquake catalogue)
  • Affected areas: Bagan and central Myanmar
  • Impacts: Major structures and ancient temples collapsed; massive cultural and economic losses
5 May 1930
Bago & Yangon
  • Magnitude: 7.2 Mw (other sources report ~7.3–7.4)
  • Affected areas: Bago, Yangon, and surrounding areas of Lower Myanmar
  • Impacts: At least 500 deaths in Bago and over 50 in Yangon; building collapses and fires; severe damage to Shwemawdaw Pagoda; ruptured a long section of the Sagaing Fault
5 July 1917
Bago
  • Magnitude: Not reliably established in historical reports
  • Affected areas: Bago and surrounding parts of Lower Myanmar
  • Impacts: Golden spire and umbrella of Shwemawdaw Pagoda fell; several smaller base pagodas damaged; no deaths reported
23 May 1912
Maymyo (Pyin Oo Lwin)
  • Magnitude: Approximately 7.6-7.7 (some report 8.0)
  • Affected areas: Northern Shan State and surrounding regions
  • Impacts: Severe damage to buildings and railway infrastructure; caused ground cracks and landslides
23 March 1839
Ava (Inwa)
  • Magnitude: Approximately 8.0
  • Affected areas: Inwa (Ava), Amarapura, Sagaing, and other parts of Myanmar
  • Impacts: Damaged houses, pagodas, monasteries, and city walls; Mingun Pagoda severely damaged; approx. 300-400 casualties
