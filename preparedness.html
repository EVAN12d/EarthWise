01 EMERGENCY KIT

Prepare an
Emergency Kit

Keep essential items ready to survive for at least three days after an earthquake.

Store enough safe drinking water for at least three days. A common guideline is about 4 litres per person per day for drinking and basic sanitation.

Keep water in clean, sealed containers and replace stored water when recommended.

Keep at least three days of non-perishable food, including canned goods, crackers, dried fruit and ready-to-eat meals.

Choose food that needs little or no cooking, and remember a manual can opener if needed.

Prepare a first aid kit with adhesive bandages, sterile gauze, medical tape, antiseptic wipes, disposable gloves and any necessary personal medications.

Check supplies regularly in case of expired or damaged items.

Keep a reliable flashlight available to help you move safely when power outages leave your surroundings dark.

Store spare batteries nearby, or consider a rechargeable flashlight with a backup power source.

A battery-powered or hand-crank radio can provide important announcements, weather information and emergency instructions.

Keep spare batteries available and check your radio regularly to ensure it works during emergencies.

Prepare copies of identification, emergency contacts, medical information, insurance details and other essential records.

Protect paper copies in a waterproof bag and keep secure digital backups when possible.

Family Emergency Plan Guide

Follow these steps to create a plan so everyone knows what to do before an earthquake happens.

Overview

An emergency plan helps you, your family, and those around you stay safe and organised before an earthquake happens. It ensures that everyone knows what to do, where to go, and how to stay in contact.

Remember

Discuss your plan with all family members, including children and elderly relatives. Practise it regularly and update it when needed.

Key Steps

1
Emergency Contacts

Make a list of important phone numbers and an out-of-area contact.

2
Meeting Locations

Choose a safe place near your home, school or neighbourhood.

3
Communication Plan

Decide how to contact each other if phone networks are down.

4
Prepare at Home

Keep emergency supplies ready and identify safe spots in each room.

Tips for a Successful Plan

Keep the plan simple and easy to remember.

Include everyone in the discussion.

Practise the plan regularly.

Review and update your plan at least every 6 months.

04 AT HOME AND SCHOOL

Be Prepared in
Your Surroundings

Take practical steps to make your home, school and other places safer.

At Home

  • Secure heavy furniture.
  • Identify safe spots in each room.
  • Keep emergency supplies ready.
  • Know how to turn off gas, water, and electricity.

At School

  • Take part in earthquake drills.
  • Follow your school's emergency plan.
  • Be aware of evacuation routes.
  • Keep your bag and area tidy, ready for quick evacuation.
